Three things about this structure, stated plainly

  • Earnings scale with the number and level of services you deliver, not with hours worked. A clinician doing four routine follow-ups in an hour earns more than one doing two. That is the trade in a per-service arrangement.
  • Your rate does not move with what the payer pays. If Medicare allows $95.19 for a 99213 and you are paid $45, you receive $45 — whether the claim collects in full, is denied and appealed, or is paid at a reduced contractual rate. It is a flat dollar figure, not a share.
  • Documentation, care coordination, inbox work, and follow-up on your own patients are yours to complete and are not separately compensated. Plan for roughly 6 to 12 minutes per encounter depending on service; the figures differ by code, and your own style will move them.

Behavioral health is a separate posting. Therapy and psychiatric services are compensated on their own rate schedule, not at the E/M rates above. If you are a therapist or psychiatrist, tell us in the application notes and we will send the correct card rather than the one on this page.

What the role looks like

  • Fully remote, anywhere in the United States
  • 1099 independent contractor — not W-2. No benefits, no employer tax withholding; you handle your own taxes, benefits and professional overhead.
  • You choose your availability and select your shifts
  • Synchronous video visits, with a smaller share of asynchronous e-visit follow-up
  • You select the level of service for your own encounters, and you complete your own documentation

What we need from you

  • An active, unrestricted license in at least one US state. Multi-state licensure or participation in the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact is a strong plus.
  • An NPI number in good standing
  • Active malpractice coverage — the specifics are below, and this is the part most candidates get wrong
  • Board certification preferred for MDs and DOs; not required for NPs and PAs
  • Previous telemedicine experience preferred, not required. We onboard clinicians who have never practised remotely.
  • A DEA registration if you will be prescribing controlled substances
  • Comfort with independent practice. There is no supervising physician in the traditional sense.

Malpractice coverage — the specifics, up front

  • You must carry your own malpractice coverage for anything you do for us, active on your first shift.
  • Minimum limits: $1,000,000 per claim / $3,000,000 aggregate.
  • Occurrence-form is strongly preferred. If you carry claims-made, you need a tail or prior-acts ("nose") endorsement covering this arrangement.
  • Coverage you hold through an employer generally does not extend to independent side work performed for another company. If your current policy excludes telemedicine or excludes moonlighting, you need separate coverage before you start.
  • Proof of coverage at credentialing, and again on any renewal, carrier change, or change in limits.

Check your policy before you apply. Employer-based coverage excluding moonlighting or telemedicine is the single most common reason a credentialed candidate cannot start.

How engagement works

US clinical engagements are structured through a physician-owned professional corporation with a management services agreement, and you would contract as an independent contractor. The payer funds the professional corporation, and your compensation is the fixed per-service rate above rather than a share of collections.

Before you sign anything: independent contractor and fee-splitting arrangements are governed by state law and vary by state, and so are the corporate practice of medicine rules under which this structure must operate. We will provide the actual agreements and the participating states for review, and we encourage you to have your own attorney review them. Nothing on this page is an offer of engagement or a contract.

Questions candidates ask most

Is this really 1099 independent contractor work?

Yes. You are engaged as an independent contractor, not an employee. There is no W-2 withholding, no benefits, and no employer contribution to your malpractice, professional liability or overhead. You are responsible for your own taxes, insurance, licenses and business expenses.

Do I get paid for credentialing time?

Credentialing is not currently compensated. License verification, malpractice proof, NPI confirmation and payer enrollment take time before your first paid shift, so plan accordingly. If that is a problem for you, this is not the right arrangement.

What happens if a claim is denied or underpaid?

Nothing changes for you. Your rate is fixed per service and is not contingent on collection. Billing, payer follow-up and appeals are handled on our side of the professional corporation.

Can I work in more than one state?

Yes, if you hold the licenses. We credential you for the states you are licensed in, and participation in the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact makes that considerably faster. Tell us your states in the application.

Do you cover behavioural health?

Yes, but on a separate rate schedule. The E/M rates on this page do not apply to therapy or psychiatric services. Note it in your application and we will send the behavioural health card.

What technology do I need?

A reliable internet connection, a quiet space for synchronous video visits, a compliant device, and a headset. The telehealth platform, scheduling, patient intake and documentation workflow are provided.

How long before I can start seeing patients?

Credentialing time is dominated by state license verification, malpractice documentation and payer enrollment. We will give you a specific timeline in writing once your application is reviewed, and we will tell you before you commit to anything if it looks long.
